Fix the Flow

Your business doesn't need another tool. It needs a better flow.

By the Experius team3 min readFix the Flow

When a business feels scattered, the instinct is to buy something: a CRM, a booking app, a chatbot, a new website. A few months later the business has one more login, and the same problems.

The observation

Most of the service businesses we talk to aren't short of tools. A typical week runs on a website form, two or three messaging apps, a shared spreadsheet, a calendar and someone's memory. Each tool works. The trouble is in the gaps between them.

An inquiry arrives on WhatsApp. Someone replies, means to follow up, and doesn't. The next week the same person fills in the website form, and a different team member starts again from scratch. Nothing is broken, and yet the customer has been let down twice.

Why it happens

Businesses grow one channel at a time. Each new channel is added to solve an immediate problem, and nobody steps back to design how information should move between them. Over time the business develops a flow nobody designed. It works only because a few people hold it together in their heads.

Adding a tool to that flow usually adds another place for information to live. Unless someone decides where things go and who owns the next step, the new tool becomes one more gap.

An example

Imagine a physiotherapy practice that adds an online booking app because the phone is always busy. Bookings improve, but patients still message on WhatsApp, and those requests are handled separately. Now the front desk checks two places, and some patients are booked twice.

The better fix wasn't the app alone. It was deciding that every request, whichever channel it came from, lands in one list, with one person responsible for confirming it. The booking app then becomes part of that flow instead of a second one. This is an illustrative example, not a client story.

The question isn't "which tool should we buy?" It's "how should information move through our business, and who owns each step?"

A practical takeaway

Pick one kind of request your business gets often: a new inquiry, an appointment, a quote. Follow the last five through your business and answer the four questions above for each step. Wherever an answer is "it depends" or "whoever sees it", you've found your first thing to fix, before buying anything.
