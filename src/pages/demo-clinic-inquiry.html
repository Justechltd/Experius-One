Every appointment request, in one place

How a practice can see every inquiry and appointment request, whichever channel it came from, with someone responsible for each one.

{{icon:info}}This is a demonstration, not a client system. All names and details are invented. It holds no patient health information.

Context

The kind of business

A multi-practitioner physiotherapy practice with a small front-desk team.

Situation

What often happens

Requests arrive by phone, WhatsApp, Facebook and the website. Each is checked by a different person, at different times.

Problem

What isn't working

Some requests get two replies, some get none, and nobody can say how many people asked this week.

The system

One list, an owner, a next action

Every request lands here automatically or is logged in one step. Personal health details stay out of it.

Experience

How the team would use it

  1. Requests arrive in one list

    Website forms flow in automatically; calls and messages are logged in one step.

  2. Each request has an owner

    Nobody wonders whether someone else has replied.

  3. Confirmations and reminders go out on their own

    Once a time is agreed, the patient gets a confirmation and a reminder.

  4. Follow-ups don't depend on memory

    After a visit, a follow-up task is created for the right person.

  5. The owner sees the week at a glance

    How many people asked, how many were booked, what's waiting.

Outcome

Nothing measured yet

This is a demonstration, so there are no results to report. In a real engagement we'd agree a baseline, then track unanswered requests, time to first reply and follow-ups completed.

Lesson

Ownership matters more than software

Most missed requests aren't caused by a missing tool. They happen because nobody is clearly responsible for the next step. The list makes ownership visible.
