From inquiry to installation, in one pipeline

How one connected record can follow every customer of a solar and battery installer, from the first inquiry to years of service, and what happens when a quote goes quiet.

Experius is a business systems and content studio. Our team is based in Bangladesh and works with service businesses locally and internationally.

Context

The kind of business

A residential and small-commercial installer with a sales team, survey technicians and installation crews.

Situation

What often happens

Inquiries come from ads, referrals and calls. Quotes are built in spreadsheets. Survey photos sit on phones. Installation is planned in chat groups.

Problem

What isn't working

Over a decision that takes weeks, nobody owns follow-up. Quotes go cold, and after handover the customer relationship ends.

The system

One pipeline, an owner and a next action for every customer

Each card is one customer record. It carries their inquiry, survey notes and photos, quote, installation date and service history.

Experience

How the team would use it

  1. Every inquiry becomes a card

    Web forms, ads and logged calls create a record automatically, with an owner.

  2. Surveys attach to the customer

    The technician uploads photos and measurements from their phone into the record.

  3. Quiet quotes surface on their own

    If a quote has no reply after a set number of days, it's flagged for follow-up.

  4. Installation is scheduled in one place

    Crew, date and parts sit on the card everyone can see.

  5. After handover, the relationship continues

    Service checks and a referral ask are scheduled automatically.

Outcome

Nothing measured yet

This is a demonstration, so there are no results to report. In a real engagement, we'd agree a baseline first and then track time to first reply, quotes followed up, installations scheduled without chasing, and service visits completed.

Lesson

The pipeline doesn't end at the sale

For installers, the customer relationship lasts for years. A system that stops at "won" misses service, warranty and referrals, which is where much of a good reputation is built.
